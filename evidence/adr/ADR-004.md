# ADR-004 — Stage 4: seating repairs (replans), closures, state schema 4

- Status: accepted (stage 4); extends ADR-001…ADR-003, which still hold
- Author: Builder
- Context: stage 4 adds manager-run seating repairs after a table closure (preview an optimal plan,
  then apply it atomically), permanent closures, series amendments (Stylist, WI-25), and import of
  stage-1, stage-2 and stage-3 exports.

## 1. The planning problem

For a proposed closure of table T over `[from, to)` at restaurant R (R-61, R-64):

- **Considered bookings** `B` are R's confirmed bookings whose occupancy overlaps `[from, to)`, on
  any table, ordered by reference. Every other confirmed booking of R is **fixed**.
- **Options** are R's singles in fixture order, then its declared pairs in declared order. An
  option's rank is its position (0-based).
- **Candidates of b ∈ B** are the options o such that:
  - `Σ b.accepted_terms.capacities[o] ≥ b.party_size`;
  - T ∉ o;
  - no applied closure on a table of o overlaps b;
  - no fixed booking overlapping b uses a table of o.
- **A plan** chooses one candidate per booking so that no two considered bookings that overlap
  in time share a table.
- **Objective** (C4.8), lexicographic:
  - (number of b whose set changes,
  - Σ unused seats,
  - rank vector in reference order).
- Limits (R-63): more than 6 tables, more than 4 pairs or more than 6 considered bookings → 422
  `planning_limit`. No plan → 409 `no_feasible_plan`.

## 2. Algorithm and optimality

`internal/planner.Solve` is an exhaustive depth-first search. It assigns bookings in reference
order and tries each booking's candidates in ascending rank. A branch is cut in two cases:

1. **Conflict.** A candidate shares a table with an earlier, time-overlapping choice. Every
   completion of that branch is infeasible, so nothing feasible is lost.
2. **Bound.** `changed + Σ(min changed of the remaining bookings)` and
   `unused + Σ(min unused of the remaining bookings)` are lower bounds for any completion. A
   branch is cut when this lower-bound pair is not lexicographically smaller than the best plan's
   (changed, unused).

**Why the bound is safe.** If the bound pair is greater, no completion can beat the best plan on
the first two levels. If it is equal, a completion can at best tie on them. Candidates are tried
in ascending rank, so leaves are reached in ascending lexicographic order of the rank vector.
Any later leaf with tied totals therefore has a larger rank vector than the incumbent and cannot
win on level 3. Every leaf that could be strictly better is still visited.

The search is complete over all combinations except those excluded by (1) or (2), so the
returned plan is the exact lexicographic optimum. The rank vector also makes the optimum
unique (ranks are distinct per option).

Size: at most 10 options per booking and 6 bookings, so at most 10^6 leaves. With the bound,
the worst case measured (10^6 equally good combinations) runs well under 100 ms. The search runs
inside the store's write lock, as one serialised step.

**Evidence.**
- `planner_test.go` compares `Solve` with an independent odometer brute force on 3,000 random
  instances.
- `state/replan_test.go` compares the whole `PlanClosure` (candidate construction included)
  with a brute force written directly from C4.7/R-64 on 1,500 random restaurants. These have up
  to 6 tables and 4 pairs, mixed single and pair bookings, cancelled bookings, reduced capacities
  in accepted terms, and earlier closures.

## 3. Preview, apply and closures

- **Preview** (`POST /restaurants/{id}/replans`) is a keyed write that stores only a `Plan`:
  - id, restaurant, closure, assignments, moved_count and unused_seats;
  - the restaurant revision at preview;
  - an applied flag.
  Nothing else changes, and the revision does not move.
- **Apply** (`…/replans/{plan_id}/apply`) checks, in one write-locked step:
  - 404 for an unknown plan or another restaurant's plan;
  - 409 `plan_already_applied`;
  - 409 `stale_plan` when the restaurant revision moved. Every write that changes R's
    occupancy, bookings, policies or series bumps R's revision (R-54/R-69), so an unchanged
    revision proves the plan's inputs are unchanged.

  It then:
  - appends the closure;
  - moves each changed booking (times and terms untouched, revision +1, one `reassigned` entry
    with a `table_ids` change and `plan_id`);
  - bumps each affected series once (exception flags untouched) and the restaurant once;
  - marks the plan applied.
  Concurrent applications serialise on the lock: exactly one succeeds, and the rest see
  `plan_already_applied` or `stale_plan`. A booking is never left half-moved.
- **Closures** are `Restaurant.Closures` and are never removed. `State.TableBusy` treats an
  applied closure like an occupying booking. Creates, PATCH, moves, adoption and series amend
  therefore answer 409 `table_unavailable` in the occupancy position. `Restaurant.Closed` serves
  availability/explain (Stylist).
- The restaurant revision is served only in the preview and apply responses.

## 4. State schema 4

- The envelope is unchanged (`format_version: 1`); `state.schema` = 4.
- `schema3to4` gives every restaurant `closures: []` and the state `plans: {}`. Series,
  histories, reservation and restaurant revisions, policies and receipts are kept (R-72).
  Earlier schemas reach 4 through the existing chain 1→2→3→4.
- `state.Rebuild` additionally checks closures (known table, `from < to`) and plans (known
  restaurant, valid closure, assignments naming bookings of that restaurant with valid sets).
- Evidence: real exports of the accepted stage-1, -2 and -3 images live in
  `stage-4/internal/snapshot/testdata`. The stage-3 export carries a series with a PATCHed
  (exception) and a cancelled occurrence. `TestUpgradeFromStage3Export` replays its receipts,
  reads its history, and repairs a closure that moves an imported series occurrence, keeping the
  exception flags.
