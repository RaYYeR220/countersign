# ADR-003 — Stage 3: dated policies, accepted terms, history, revisions, state schema 3

- Status: accepted (stage 3); extends ADR-001 and ADR-002, which still hold
- Author: Builder
- Context: the stage-3 specification adds dated booking policies published by restaurant
  managers, a per-reservation revision and accepted terms, an owner-only history and decision
  record, recurring series, and the requirement to import stage-1 and stage-2 exports.

## 1. Policies and their selection

- `Restaurant.Policies []Policy` holds the published policies in publication order. A policy's
  version is its position + 1, so versions are dense and never reused. A policy is a
  `Terms` value (version, grid, duration, cutoff, opening hours, capacities per table) plus its
  `effective_from`. Policies are immutable once appended.
- Policy 0 is never stored. `Restaurant.Policy0()` derives it from the fixture fields, so the
  restaurant detail (`GET /restaurants/{id}`) keeps serving exactly the fixture configuration.
- **One selection function** — `(*Restaurant).PolicyFor(localDate)` returns the greatest
  `effective_from` not later than the date, ties to the greatest version, and policy 0 otherwise.
  Availability (with explain), booking, amendment, moves and series occurrences all call it, so
  they can never disagree.
- Publication (`POST /restaurants/{id}/policies`) is an idempotent keyed write (ADR-001 §4).
  Parse, permission check, append and receipt happen in one write-locked step. A failure or a
  replay therefore allocates no version, and concurrent publications get consecutive versions.

## 2. Accepted terms and revisions

- Every reservation stores `Terms` (the selected policy's terms at acceptance, without
  `effective_from`) and `Revision` (1 at creation). `EndsAt` is computed from the accepted
  duration, so publishing a policy never moves an existing booking's end.
- Cancel and amendment check the **accepted** cutoff against the current start.
- A real amendment (tables, start or party differ; a reversed pair is the same set) is validated
  in full against the policy of the resulting start date, adopts its terms and end time, and
  increments the revision once. A no-op keeps terms, end and revision, but still requires a
  confirmed booking outside its cutoff.
- `expected_revision` (PATCH and move items) is checked before cancelled/cutoff/values. The
  check and the change run in the same write-locked step, so of N concurrent amendments sharing
  one revision at most one real change commits.
- Planning and writing are separate (`planChange` → `conflicts` → `apply`). `apply` is the
  only writer for PATCH and moves: it bumps each changed booking's revision, records its history
  entry, marks series exceptions, and bumps each affected series and the restaurant once per
  operation. Nothing is written if any check fails.

## 3. History and decision

- `Reservation.History` is an append-only list; `seq` is the list position + 1, so it is dense
  and totally ordered, even within one second. `Reservation.Record` appends an entry carrying
  the resulting revision and a copy of the accepted terms, so old entries keep their old terms.
- `created` lists all three fields from null. `changed` lists only changed fields, in the order
  table, start, party. Table changes use `table_id` between single tables and `table_ids`
  (complete lists in declared order) whenever a pair is involved. `cancelled` has empty changes.
  No-ops, failures and replays record nothing.
- `GET …/history` and `GET …/decision` read under the read lock. Anyone but the owner (including
  an anonymous caller) gets the same 404.

## 4. Restaurant revision

`Restaurant.Revision` is a persisted counter: +1 per committed write touching the restaurant —
booking creation, real amendment, cancel (not a repeat), a move batch (once), a series adoption
(once) and a policy publication. It is not served by any endpoint unless the Foreman rules
otherwise (escalation E7).

## 5. State schema 3 and migrations

The envelope stays `{"track":"tablekeeper","format_version":1}` and `state.schema` becomes 3.
Import migrates step by step (ADR-001 §6). `schema1to2` (ADR-002) is unchanged. `schema2to3`:

- restaurants gain `manager_user_ids: []`, `policies: []`, `revision: 0`;
- each reservation gains `revision: 1`, the accepted terms of its restaurant's policy 0, a
  history holding one `created` entry stamped with its `created_at`, and `series_id: ""` —
  cancelled ones included (escalation E6);
- the state gains an empty `series` collection;
- users, tokens, ids, references, statuses, timestamps and receipts (with their original response
  bytes) are carried over unchanged. A stage-1 or stage-2 retry therefore replays its original
  response, without the stage-3 fields.

`state.Rebuild` now also checks managers (known users, distinct), policies (dense versions, valid
dates and terms with capacities naming exactly the restaurant's tables), every reservation's
revision, terms and dense history ending at its revision, and series integrity (owner,
2..12 occurrences, mutual membership).

Evidence: `stage-3/internal/snapshot/testdata/stage1-export.json` and `stage2-export.json` are
real exports of the accepted stage-1 and stage-2 images. The tests import both and check terms,
history, sessions, byte-identical replays, failed keys as first uses, and amendments of imported
bookings.
